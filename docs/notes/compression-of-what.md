## The claim

The engine's founding frame: a constraint store is branching compressed
into data — what would be potentially exponential branching held as one
factor and computed optimally instead of searched. The store arc forced
the question that frame had left open: compression of WHAT?

Of operations that work on substitutions. A domain x ∈ {1..5} is five
substitution extensions held as one value; a disequality record is the
complement of one. Every store is a compressed set of ALTERNATIVES, and an
alternative is an operation on the substitution.

The substitution itself holds no alternatives — it holds just the
REALIZED value, the one branch that is actual. It is not a "proper"
alternative, so it cannot be one of the compressed things: it is the
thing the compressions operate ON. That is the privilege, and it is
ontological, not historical. Every attempt to file it among the stores
produced the same symptoms, now recognizable as diagnostic rather than
accidental: no-op reactions (it cannot revise — revisions are defined
against it), empty projections (it has no alternatives to project),
an exclusion filter at every dispatch site (the store operations
quantify over alternatives; the substitution is what they are
alternatives TO).

This is why the arc got nowhere: substitutions were still privileged, and
the interface could only restate the privilege as special cases. The July
floor/tile ruling was right for a reason July did not have words for —
the floor is what the tiles are laid on.
