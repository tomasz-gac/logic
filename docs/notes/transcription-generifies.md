## The claim

Every hand-written `Projectable.rename` is an instance of ONE generic
transcription: terms rename through the Renaming, ground data rides
unchanged, and closures rebuild positionally from the renamed terms.
Content in the (actuals, template) shape — arguments as terms plus a
maker whose closure is excluded from identity — crosses every boundary
with no knowledge of its own structure: rename the actuals, regenerate
the item. `Stored` never needs a transcription face; future stores owe
nothing; the nogoods-ride-tabling stage needs no per-store code.

The proof of universality is the double negation: a nogood holding a
statement literal is itself a `Stored`, statable inside another nogood —
if negation-wrapped content crosses, anything literal-shaped does. The
double wrapper is the proof, NOT a transport mechanism: moving a
positive constraint is just restating its literal (the self-restating
factors pattern), and ¬¬C trades C's eager propagation for an inner
nogood's lazy veto — semantically adjacent, operationally weaker.

## The boundary

The value semantics never generify. How a store combines and orders its
own knowledge — meet, leq, normalize — is custody-owned and stays
behind the store wall; the (actuals, template) shape transports
contents, it does not interpret them. Projectable's transcription half
dissolves into the generic mechanism; its algebra half stays where it
lives. Substitutions remain the floor, per the standing ruling.

## What it would dissolve, if the kill-check survives

Per-store `rename` implementations (Neq's `renamedRecord`, the nogood
store's own, every future store's) collapse into the one mechanism over
exposed contents; `split` is already generic given `terms()` coverage.
A store's Projectable face reduces to exposing its records — the
transcription is nobody's code.
